B2. SR-6 WITHDRAWAL TRACE

1. The owner queues a positive amount of a listed NEP141 asset to an approved receiver, with 1 yoctoNEAR and an 86,400-second delay. Vault and configured Intents receivers reject. Replacement restarts the delay; cancellation advances request generation.
2. Anyone may apply a matured request. The method checks trading, signature and withdrawal state, and requires at least 170 TGas. It removes the queue and stores a uniquely identified active operation before reading the baseline ledger balance.
3. The private callback before sending requires matching ID and no stored baseline. Failed/malformed/insufficient read can safely fail/requeue only unchanged authorization because nothing was sent. A stale read after recovery cannot send.
4. The baseline is stored before ft_withdraw(token, receiver_id, amount), which receives 1 yoctoNEAR. Token suffix uses real AccountId grammar; only NEP141 recovery accepted.
5. Returning the exact full amount records Delivered and clears the guard. Explicit zero records the refund demonstrated by the trusted fixture, clears the guard and permits a generation-matched retry. Failed returned promise, oversized/malformed parse or positive partial result: remain unresolved; never duplicate the old amount.
6. Recovery after 300 seconds: pre-send timeout cancels safely and may requeue. Sent phase reads ledger; only full observed debit clears as DebitObserved, not proof of receiver delivery. Unchanged/increased/deposit-masked balance stays guarded (F26).
7. A late callback carrying an old operation ID cannot resolve a newer retry or resurrect cancelled/replaced authorization. Queuing/cancelling new owner work does not revoke an already dispatched transfer.

Full delivery and FT failure followed by an Intents zero-result refund and a retry of the same authorized request under a new operation ID are tested through actual sandbox receipts. The token remains deliberately minimal, with a test-only failure hook; it does not model production storage/metadata/fees. Lost/malformed terminal outcomes are unit injected, not live finality proof.

B4. NINE ENGINE GATES, IN ORDER

1. Structure: the assets differ, both are allowed, and amount_in and min_out are positive.
2. Outstanding signature and cooldown: now >= signed_expiry and, after a prior trade, now >= last_trade + interval.
3. Prices: every traded asset and every asset with a positive holding has a usable price; publication <= now + 5 seconds and age <= max_age. Incoherent adapter data can reject earlier as MissingPrice.
4. Balance: the input holding is at least amount_in.
5. Size: rounded-up notional is at most max_trade_usd.
6. Turnover: the snapshot UTC day starts at now - now % 86,400. Use the same-day counter or zero; saturated addition must remain within the daily cap.
7. Slippage: floor(output USD) * 10,000 >= ceil(input USD) * (10,000 - slippage_bps), using wide multiplication.
8. Drawdown: compare NAV with max(previous HWM, NAV). Falling below HWM * (1 - drawdown) permits only configured stable destinations.
9. Position: output addition is checked and NAV_after must be positive. The non-stable destination’s worst-case exposure and other non-stable positions whose shares rise must remain within the cap. An overweight position whose share falls during de-risking may remain above the cap.

First failure ends evaluation. Stable destination skips neither prior gates nor other-position share-rise checks. Freshness does not prove price correctness/peg. Direct donations and later market/settlement state are outside the snapshot.
